01 / OVERVIEW
About the ISI

Establishment

The Inter-Services Intelligence was established in 1948, shortly after Pakistan's independence. Originally tasked with coordinating intelligence between the Pakistani military branches, it evolved into one of the most powerful and secretive intelligence agencies in the world. Within this MILSIM context, ISI represents the most elite and clandestine tier of the Pakistani defense apparatus.

Mandate

ISI's mandate spans foreign intelligence gathering, counter-intelligence, counter-terrorism operations, and special covert operations. The Covert Actions Division is the sharpest instrument in that toolkit — handling missions that are deniable, sensitive, and of the highest national security priority. Operators work across all environments, often without recognition or attribution.

Structure

ISI is headed by a Director General (DG) holding the rank of Lieutenant General. Below the DG, the organization is divided into multiple directorates handling different aspects of intelligence and covert operations. The Covert Actions Division operates under a separate chain of command with enhanced operational autonomy and is accountable directly to senior leadership.

Operational Philosophy

ISI operates on a principle of strategic ambiguity — neither confirming nor denying involvement in operations. Operators are trained to maintain cover under all circumstances, operate with minimal footprint, and achieve objectives through precision rather than force of numbers. Discipline, discretion, and adaptability are non-negotiable traits.

Special Operations Wing

The direct action arm of the Covert Actions Division. Operators from W-I conduct targeted raids, high-value individual elimination or capture, and crisis intervention missions. Teams are small (4–6 operators), fast-moving, and trained to operate far behind adversary lines. All personnel are cross-trained in breaching, close-quarters combat, and SERE.

CAD // W-III
Human Intelligence Wing

HUMINT operatives are ISI's eyes and ears on the ground in denied territories. W-III manages agent networks, conducts undercover infiltration, and builds relationships with key foreign contacts. Operators maintain deep cover for extended periods, often living entirely within their cover identity. This wing is the foundation upon which all other CAD operations are built — without reliable intelligence, no mission succeeds.

CAD // W-IV
Counter-Intelligence Wing

W-IV is tasked with protecting ISI operations from penetration by foreign intelligence services. This wing investigates potential moles, conducts internal security audits, and runs double-agent programs to feed disinformation to adversaries. Counter-intelligence operators are among the most analytically rigorous in the entire Division — their work is often invisible, but always critical.

CAD // W-V
Psychological Operations Wing

Information warfare, narrative control, and influence operations. W-V operators craft and disseminate strategic messaging to shape adversary decision-making, public opinion, and battlefield perception. Working at the intersection of intelligence, media, and psychology, this wing conducts operations that are often the most consequential — and the least visible. Domestic operations are strictly prohibited; all PSYOP activities target external audiences.

Requirements

✓ Mandatory Requirements

  • Active member of the MILSIM group or affiliated unit in good standing
  • Minimum rank of E-4 / Corporal or equivalent, depending on branch of service
  • No active disciplinary actions, strikes, or pending investigations
  • Must be vouched for or recommended by a current ISI member or officer
  • Agreement to abide by all ISI operational security (OPSEC) protocols
  • Willingness to operate under strict command hierarchy and follow orders without hesitation
  • Demonstrated activity and engagement within the broader group

★ Preferred Qualifications

  • Prior experience in a special operations or intelligence role in any MILSIM group
  • Strong communication skills and ability to write clear, structured after-action reports
  • Familiarity with covert operations doctrine and intelligence tradecraft
  • Ability to participate regularly in scheduled operations and training events
  • Demonstrated maturity, discretion, and ability to maintain confidentiality
  • Leadership experience or ability to independently command a small team
  • Experience with roleplay-heavy or scenario-driven MILSIM environments
